1. Executive Summary & The Problem

The Farmer's Crisis (Debt Trap)

• 86% of Indian farmers are small/marginal (<2 hectares).

• Formal bank credit requires heavy collateral and bureaucratic approval cycles that take 3–6 weeks — arriving too late for optimal sowing windows.

• Farmers resort to local moneylenders charging 24% to 36% compound annual interest.

• In drought or pest attacks, debt compounds, leading to land mortgage loss and severe distress.

The Investor's Dilemma

• Retail and HNI investors hold over ₹65 Lakh Crore in low-yielding fixed deposits and volatile stocks.

• Agriculture offers 18–32% cyclical real-asset returns, but investors face zero access, opaque middlemen, and fears of agrarian disputes.

• The Gap: No institutional bridge exists providing legally binding, insured, satellite-monitored crop investment agreements.

Land Ownership Protection (Section 5(1) MLRC)

Ironclad Legal Guarantee: Investors NEVER acquire any rights, liens, or claims over the farmer's land. The Tripartite Agreement explicitly restricts investor rights to the economic proceeds of the specific crop harvest. Section 5(1) of the Maharashtra Land Revenue Code protects agricultural land against alienation to non-agriculturists.

6. Deep-Dive: The Tripartite Agreement & Smart Escrow

1
Farmer Listing Creation: Farmer enters crop type, acreage, working capital needed (e.g. ₹50,000), tenure (120 days), and proposed profit split (70% Farmer / 30% Investor).
2
Investor Backing & Legal E-Sign: Investor browses listings, picks Grade A Nashik Red Onion, selects investment amount (min ₹5,000), reviews the auto-generated Tripartite Crop Investment Agreement, and e-signs via OTP.
3
Capital Deposited in Escrow Account: Funds do not go directly into the farmer's personal savings account. They are held in a scheduled bank escrow account operated by Bharat Agri-Financial Services.
4
3-Tranche Milestone Disbursement:
  • Tranche 1 (40% - ₹20,000): Disbursed upon land tilling and verified input purchase (seeds & base fertilizer).
  • Tranche 2 (35% - ₹17,500): Disbursed at 45 days post-germination upon field agent geo-tagged photo & NDVI verification.
  • Tranche 3 (25% - ₹12,500): Disbursed at 80 days pre-harvest for labour, pest-spray, and harvest crates.
5
Harvest, Direct Sale & Waterfall Split: The produce is sold directly to Fresh Basket Retail at ₹2,200/quintal. Gross proceeds (₹88,000) are routed back into the Escrow.

12. Top 5 Tough Investor Questions & Answers

Q1: What happens if there is a severe drought or hailstorm and the crop fails completely?

Answer: Unlike bank loans where the farmer is ruined, FasalSanjha integrates dual insurance: 1) Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY) covering yield loss for the farmer, and 2) Parametric private capital protection cover for investors (triggered by verified rainfall/satellite deficits), returning 50% to 70% of invested principal. The remaining loss is shared according to the tripartite equity contract.

Q2: What prevents the farmer from selling the harvest in the black market and claiming it was lost?

Answer: Three layers of tamper-proof verification: 1) Weekly automated Sentinel-2 satellite NDVI scans flag pre-harvest harvesting activities in real time; 2) Local cluster field agents visit 10 days before harvest to estimate tonnage; 3) All transport from farm gate must be booked through FasalSanjha Logistics with GPS e-way bills to unlock final tranche payouts.

Q3: Is this considered collective investment or NBFC lending by RBI / SEBI?

Answer: No. FasalSanjha operates as a technology facilitator for bilateral / tripartite Agricultural Production Agreements under the Indian Contract Act, 1872. There is no pooled fund, no guaranteed debt return, and money stays in a scheduled bank escrow account until milestone conditions are verified.

Q4: How do you acquire farmers in rural India?

Answer: Through local FPOs (Farmer Producer Organizations), village Krishi Seva Kendras, and our commissioned field agents (local agri-diploma graduates who earn 1% on verified settlements). We don't acquire individual farmers cold; we onboard entire 50-farmer clusters at once.

Q5: What is your primary competitive advantage against DeHaat, Ninjacart, or Samunnati?

Answer: Ninjacart focuses solely on B2B supply chain logistics; DeHaat focuses on input retail; Samunnati provides debt NBFC loans. FasalSanjha is the ONLY platform connecting retail/institutional capital directly to crop equity, aligning the economic incentives of farmer, investor, and buyer in a closed loop.
